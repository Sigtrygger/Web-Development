Reflection: "7 Cures for a Lean Purse"

In George S. Clason's The Richest Man in Babylon, Arkad outlines seven fundamental steps to attain financial security. The cure that resonated most with me is the first: "Control thy expenditures"."

Trying to balance a job with university classes means I have a lot of bills to pay, and I’ve seen for myself how fast a paycheck can disappear if I'm not paying attention. The book makes a great point that if you don't budget, your "necessary expenses" will always grow to match whatever you earn unless you put your foot down.

For me at the moment, saving money isn't just some abstract advice, it's a daily challenge. Between transportation, food, and regular living expenses, it’s dangerously easy to spend all my earnings without realizing. Taking control of my spending means being honest with myself about what I actually need versus what I just want in the moment. By keeping a tighter grip on my casual spending, I can actually start putting that 10% away for myself, rather than working all month just to end up exactly where I started.

Reflection: "5 Laws of Gold"

The "5 Laws of Gold" had me really thinking about how we protect our money, especially the warnings against get rich quick schemes. When you’re a student thats also working and paying for bills/tuition, you’re tempted to look for quick money, especially when you see people online talking about fast cash.

However the book gives a reality check and a harsh truth, Money disappears fast when you chase after impossible returns or listen to the advice of inexperienced people. My biggest takeaway is that if I’m working hard to save part of my paycheck, I can’t afford to gamble it. Instead of searching for a quick payout, I need to be patient, protect what I earn and take financial advice only from people with a track record.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest overall takeaway from the book is that financial security is more about your daily practices than your paycheck. If I can't manage my money well enough now on a tight working student budget, then getting a bigger paycheck after graduation isn't going to solve my spending problems, it's just going to mean I have more money to burn up. First, i must discipline myself by learning to control my expenses and protect what little I can save today.
