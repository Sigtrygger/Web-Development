Critical Reflection: LLMs as a Tool, Not a Crutch

When people say tools like ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot should be used as a “tool and not a crutch”, it really depends on whether you’re trying to learn or you’re just trying to get an assignment done. A tool is something that helps you work smarter, like checking your work on a calculator. A crutch is something you lean on when you don't want to do the heavy lifting yourself. If I just let an AI write my code or draft my paragraphs, I completely skip the mental struggle that actually helps things stick (Bjork & Bjork, 2011)

When, Where, and How to Use LLMs

  • When: When I'm genuinely stuck on a confusing error message or a weird syntax bug. Instead of asking it to write the whole script, I ask it to explain why a specific line fails or to break down a concept into plain English.
  • Where: For brainstorming and quick reviews
  • How: Instead of asking for answers, I tell it to act like a tutor. Asking things like "Don't give me the solution, walk me through the logic step-by-step" keeps me engaged in solving the problem myself (Mollick & Mollick, 2023).

In the end, an AI-produced clean result creates a false sense of confidence. You may think you know it, but that goes out the window when you are in an exam room without Wi-Fi, or trying to explain your code in an interview.
